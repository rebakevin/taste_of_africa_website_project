A short history

Africa's cuisines grew from the land: millet, sorghum, yams and teff were cultivated for thousands of years before trade routes carried cloves, cumin and rice across the Sahara and the Indian Ocean. Each exchange left a mark in the pot.

The Columbian exchange brought tomatoes, chillies and cassava, which became the backbone of dishes we now think of as ancient. Later, the forced migration of millions carried West African techniques to the Caribbean and the Americas, shaping gumbo, feijoada and soul food.

Today, a new generation of cooks, farmers and chefs is reclaiming indigenous grains, fermentation and open-fire cooking, and the world is finally tasting it.
